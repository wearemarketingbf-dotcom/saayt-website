A one-star review on your branch shows up for everyone who searches for you, and the first question is usually how to delete it. The honest answer: a business owner can't delete a review, and Google won't remove a review just because it's negative. Google does remove reviews that break its policies, and it gives you an official way to report and appeal.

This guide covers when a review gets removed and when it doesn't, how to report it step by step, and what to do about a genuine negative review.

## The short answer: negative isn't a reason

Google is direct about this: "Do not report a review just because you disagree with it or dislike it." Only reviews that violate content policies are eligible for removal, and reported reviews that do violate them "will no longer show on Maps and Search" ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773?hl=en)).

So the useful question isn't "is this review unfair?" It's "does it break a specific policy?"

## Review types that break Google's policies

Google has a content policy for everything users post on Maps. Key prohibited categories include ([Google](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en)):

- **Fake engagement:** for example, reviews paid for directly or in kind
- **Conflict of interest:** Google gives current or former employment as an example
- **Off-topic:** content not based on an experience at the place, such as general political or social commentary
- **Harassment, hate speech and offensive content**
- **Personal information**
- **Obscenity and profanity**
- **Advertising and solicitation**
- **Unclear and repetitive content**

If a review doesn't fall into one of these categories, it will most likely stay up, however harsh it feels.

## How to report a review, step by step

From your Business Profile ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773?hl=en)):

1. Go to your Business Profile and select **Read reviews**
2. Next to the review, select **Report**
3. Choose a reason, such as "Spam" or "Profanity"
4. Select **Send report**

Our advice before reporting: pick the reason that genuinely matches the violation, and keep a screenshot of the review and its date in an internal log so you can follow up.

## The Reviews Management Tool: tracking and appeals

Google's Reviews Management Tool lets you report reviews and track their status. You confirm the account, select the business, choose **Report a new review for removal**, pick a reason and submit ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773?hl=en)).

What Google says about timing and statuses:

- **Timing:** review evaluation typically takes several days
- **Decision pending:** flagged but not yet evaluated
- **Report reviewed, no policy violation:** Google found no violation. You can submit a one-time appeal
- **Escalated:** your appeal is under review, and the final decision arrives by email

On appeals: you get one appeal for a review that wasn't removed, and you can select up to 10 reviews per appeal. Results arrive by email. Violating reviews are removed and compliant ones stay live.

Because the appeal is one-time, don't rush it. Make your case clearly before you use it.

### If you're being extorted over reviews

If someone demands money to stop posting negative reviews, don't engage. Google has a separate reporting guide for extortion scams, linked from its review removal page ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773?hl=en)).

## Why genuine reviews sometimes disappear

You may notice a real, positive review from a customer has vanished. Google says its automated system may remove reviews as spam and can sometimes remove legitimate ones. If that happens, Google suggests contacting support ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773?hl=en)).

There's another reason worth knowing. If Google finds fake or incentivised reviews on a profile, it may apply restrictions beyond removing those reviews. Examples Google lists ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14114287?hl=en)):

- The profile can't receive new reviews or ratings for a set period
- Existing reviews or ratings are unpublished for a set period
- The profile displays a warning telling consumers that fake reviews were removed

Google says it emails the business owner before applying a restriction, and the owner can appeal. Buying reviews or offering something in return doesn't solve a negative review. It puts the whole profile at greater risk.

## What never to do

- Don't offer a customer a discount or gift to change or remove their review. Google's policy prohibits it explicitly ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en))
- Don't ask staff to post reviews to bury a negative one. Google treats reviews tied to employment as a conflict of interest
- Don't report every negative review without a clear reason. It wastes your time and won't change the outcome
- Don't ask only happy customers for reviews. Google's policy prohibits selectively soliciting positive reviews ([Google](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en))

## The better alternative: a professional reply future customers will read

A genuine negative review won't be removed, but your reply is in your hands. Future customers read the review and your reply together. A calm reply that apologises and offers to resolve things privately says more about your business than the review itself.

Remember that the reviewer is notified when you reply and can edit their review after reading it ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050?hl=en)). Any change should be their own choice after the problem is solved, never in exchange for anything.

## Checklist: when a negative review arrives

- Read it twice and note the branch and date
- Ask: does it break a specific Google policy?
- If yes: report it, choose the most accurate reason and keep a copy
- If no: don't report it. Write a short, professional reply
- Apologise where it's due and move details to phone or email
- Check internally with the branch manager what happened
- Track the report status in the Reviews Management Tool after a few days
- Use your one appeal only when your case is clear
- Log negative reviews by branch so repeat problems stand out

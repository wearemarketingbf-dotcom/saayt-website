Every review on your Google Business Profile has two readers: the customer who wrote it, and every future customer who reads the review and your reply before deciding to visit. A good reply doesn't change what happened, but it shows how your business treats people.

This guide collects what Google officially says about replying to reviews, with practical examples for each review type and a simple way to organise replies across many branches.

## Why replying to every review matters

On its local ranking page, Google says that replying to customer reviews "shows that you value their feedback", and that "positive reviews and helpful replies can help your business stand out" ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en)). Its review management page adds that helpful and positive replies "can show that you're responsive to your customers" ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050?hl=en)).

Note what Google does not say: it doesn't promise that replying raises your rating or your ranking. The goal is trust and clarity, not a number.

## Before you start: who can reply

- **Verify first:** Google says you must verify your business before you can reply to reviews
- **Replies show as the business:** your personal name isn't shown
- **Owners and managers:** both roles can respond to reviews ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3403100?hl=en))
- **The reviewer is notified:** they get a notification when you reply, and they can still edit their review after reading it

Source for all of the above except roles: [Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050?hl=en).

## How to reply, step by step

1. Go to your Business Profile
2. Select **Read reviews**
3. Next to the review, select **Reply**
4. Enter your reply in the dialog box
5. Select **Reply** to send

You can edit or delete a reply later by selecting **Edit** under it ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050?hl=en)).

## Google's official tips for a good reply

These come from Google's own tips page ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en)):

- **Keep it short:** customers value direct and honest responses, and long replies may not be read to the end
- **Don't send the same "thank you" to everyone:** tailor the reply to what the customer wrote
- **Be conversational, not promotional:** avoid offering deals or promotions in your reply
- **Protect privacy:** never share a reviewer's private information or engage in personal attacks
- **Take details offline:** for complex issues, politely ask the reviewer to contact you by phone or email
- **Apologise when it's due:** a genuine apology can go a long way in rebuilding trust
- **Reply promptly:** a prompt response shows you value the customer's feedback

## Example replies for each review type

Treat these as starting points, not templates to paste. Change the details to match what the customer wrote so your replies don't all sound the same.

### Positive review with details

The customer wrote that the team at the Olaya branch was quick.

> Thank you for the kind words about our Olaya team. We're glad your visit was quick and easy, and we look forward to seeing you again

Why it works: it names the branch and the detail the customer mentioned, and it's short.

### Negative review

The customer complained about a long wait.

> We're sorry about the wait you had. That's not the standard we want for our customers. Please contact us on the number in our profile so we can understand what happened and fix it

Why it works: a clear apology without a long excuse, a move to a private channel as Google suggests, and no offer of anything in exchange for changing the review.

### Rating with no text

A star rating with no words needs only a short reply.

> Thanks for your rating. We'd be glad to hear any feedback whenever you like

For a low rating with no text:

> Thank you for your time. We'd like to know how we can serve you better. Please reach us on the number in our profile

### Review written in dialect

When a customer writes in Saudi dialect, reply in a register close to theirs without overdoing it. In the Arabic version of this guide we show a short reply in light Saudi dialect. The rule is the same in any language: clear and respectful, matched to the customer's tone, never sloppy.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Offers in replies:** Google advises against using replies for deals or promotions
- **Incentives to change a review:** Google prohibits offering incentives in exchange for posting a review, changing one or removing a negative one ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en))
- **Exposing customer details:** don't mention full names, order numbers or visit details in a public reply
- **Copy-paste replies:** the same sentence on dozens of reviews reads as automated
- **Arguing in public:** even when the customer is wrong, future readers judge your tone

### A note for clinics and medical centres

Our advice: in a public reply, avoid anything that confirms the person was a patient or mentions their treatment or condition. Thank them and invite them to a private channel. This applies Google's rule against sharing private information directly.

## How long a reply takes to appear, and why it may be rejected

Google says replies usually take up to 10 minutes to review, but sometimes it can take up to 30 days. Google checks each reply against its content policies, and if a reply isn't approved you're asked to edit it ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050?hl=en)).

If your reply doesn't show up right away, don't post it again. Wait, or check whether it breaks a policy.

## Organising replies across several branches

With five or more branches, the questions become who replies, when, and in what tone. Practical steps:

1. **Name one approver:** anyone can draft a reply, but one named person approves it before it goes live
2. **Agree a response time:** for example, every review answered within one business day
3. **Write a short tone guide:** how we apologise, how we thank, when we move to a private channel
4. **Mention the branch in the reply:** readers can tell the reply is personal, not a template
5. **Review negative reviews weekly by branch:** a repeat issue at one branch is an operations problem, not a reply problem
6. **Assign roles carefully:** add branch managers as Managers, not Owners. Only owners can add and remove users ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3403100?hl=en))
